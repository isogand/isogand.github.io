Research

LPECO: a control-inspired optimizer for tabular neural networks

With Siavash Mobarhan. Preprint, 2025.

LPECO adds an integral, PI-control-inspired correction term to the Lion optimizer. The term is meant to damp excess momentum before the sign step. We compared a ResNet-like network trained with LPECO against Adam, AdamW, Lion, LightGBM, and XGBoost on eight tabular datasets, using the same 128-trial hyperparameter search for every pairing.

On this benchmark, LPECO was statistically equivalent to LightGBM (TOST) and had a 98.4% posterior probability of practical equivalence to XGBoost (Bayesian ROPE), and it obtained the highest mean rank. On the BreastCancer dataset, its distribution of results was higher than both tree models (Mann-Whitney U, p < 0.001).

Limitations: eight datasets, one architecture, one random seed, accuracy only, and the omnibus Friedman test was not significant (p = 0.422). The equivalence margin comes from our own benchmark, and the correction term is a heuristic without a convergence analysis. A link between LPECO's advantage and class balance was only a non-significant trend (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.54, p = 0.170).

Emergent Brittleness: prompt strategy and model choice in LLM code optimization

With Siavash Mobarhan, supervised by H. R. Hamidi (IKIU). Preprint, 2025.

We ran a 3×3×5 factorial experiment (270 runs) with three LLMs, three prompt strategies, and five synthetic Python optimization tasks. Correctness was checked with unit tests and analyzed with a mixed-effects logistic model; performance was analyzed with generalized least squares.

An elaborate “expert” prompt significantly reduced correctness for gemini-2.5-flash-lite (p = 0.030) and showed a non-significant trend for qwen-2.5-72b-instruct (p = 0.098). A persona prompt showed a trend toward slower solutions for qwen-2.5-72b-instruct (p = 0.059). In short, the effect of a prompt depended on the model.

Limitations: the expert prompt bundles role, output format, a reflective workflow, and quality constraints, so I cannot say which component matters. Tasks were synthetic, and several effects are trends, not confirmed results. Next steps are component ablations, more runs, and public benchmarks.
